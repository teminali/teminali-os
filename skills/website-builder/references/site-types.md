# Website Types and Structures

Choose the closest type from the user's actual goal. These are starting structures,
not mandatory page templates; remove sections that do not advance the primary
action.

## Conversion landing pages

### SaaS or product

- Goal: start a trial, book a demo, or adopt a product.
- Typical flow: focused hero, concrete outcome, product demonstration, key use
  cases, proof, objection handling, pricing or next step, final call to action.
- Prioritize product evidence over abstract feature cards.

### Service or lead generation

- Goal: produce a qualified inquiry or booking.
- Typical flow: audience/problem fit, differentiated offer, process, representative
  work, proof, qualification details, FAQ, contact action.
- State geography, availability, scope, and response expectations when known.

### Waitlist or pre-launch

- Goal: capture informed interest without overstating an unfinished product.
- Typical flow: concise promise, intended audience, credible preview, expected
  availability, privacy-aware signup, founder or project context.
- Do not fabricate adoption, release dates, or product screenshots.

Before writing claims, maintain a compact register:

| Claim | Status | Evidence source | Intended location | Owner/gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact statement | verified / prototype / planned / unknown | Supplied artifact or fact | Page/section | Who must resolve it |

Prefer credibility in this order: real product evidence, reviewed technical or
security facts, relevant founder expertise, transparent beta terms and roadmap,
then externally supplied customer proof. Label prototypes and planned capabilities
in the interface; never promote them silently to shipped features.

### Application or download

- Goal: install, download, or begin onboarding.
- Typical flow: immediate product view, platform compatibility, core jobs, workflow
  demonstration, privacy/security facts, ratings or proof when supplied, download
  action.

### Event or campaign

- Goal: register, attend, donate, or participate before a deadline.
- Typical flow: essential event facts, value, speakers/program, logistics, proof,
  FAQ, repeated registration action.
- Keep date, time zone, location, eligibility, and price unambiguous.

### Product or direct-to-consumer

- Goal: understand and purchase one featured product.
- Typical flow: product and price, benefit demonstration, variants, materials or
  specifications, proof, shipping/returns facts, FAQ, purchase action.
- Treat cart, tax, inventory, and checkout as real integrations, not visual props.

## Multi-page websites

### Company or startup

- Core pages: Home, Product or Services, About, proof/resources, Contact.
- Home should route distinct audiences rather than duplicate every child page.

### Agency or professional service

- Core pages: Home, Services, Work/Case Studies, About, Contact.
- Case studies should explain problem, contribution, result, and evidence—not only
  image galleries.

### Local business

- Core pages: Home, offerings/menu, location or service area, About, Contact.
- Surface hours, phone, address, accessibility, booking, and local trust details.

### Portfolio or personal brand

- Core pages: concise Home, selected Work, About, Contact; add writing only if it is
  maintained.
- Lead with judgment and outcomes, not tool lists or decorative skill meters.

### Editorial or content site

- Core pages: Home, topic/index pages, article detail, About, search or archive.
- Prioritize reading hierarchy, navigation, metadata, and resilient long-form
  layouts.

### Documentation or developer site

- Core pages: overview, quickstart, concepts, task guides, reference, troubleshooting.
- Optimize for findability, copyable examples, version clarity, and a short path to
  first success.

## Selection rules

- Use one primary website type and add secondary patterns only when the audience
  journey requires them.
- Distinguish a landing page from a full marketing site before deciding navigation
  and page count.
- Build commerce, authentication, payments, search, CMS, or booking as functional
  systems only when they are in scope; otherwise label the boundary clearly.
